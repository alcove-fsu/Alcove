01 The problem

Loneliness is a health problem, not just a sad one.

Social isolation among older adults is linked to depression, cognitive decline, poorer chronic disease outcomes, and higher mortality risk.

Volunteering and community participation are a proven way back. But the people who need them most are often the hardest to reach: older adults living alone in rural, minority, and low-income communities. For them, the opportunities are hard to find, hard to get to, and hard to keep up with. Most digital tools hand people a list and walk away.

ALCOVE is built for what happens after the list: understanding what someone actually enjoys, what they can physically manage, how they get around, and what stops them from going, and then staying in the conversation.

02 The system

How ALCOVE works

Four steps, in order, every time someone asks for something to do.

It learns what fits

A short, plain-language conversation captures interests, physical ability, transportation access, social preferences, and personal goals: asked one question at a time, not as a form.

It searches real local opportunities

A curated database of North Florida activities, built from public listings and entered directly by senior centers, nonprofits, and faith-based groups, then reviewed and validated so the details are current and correct.

It checks its own work

Before any suggestion reaches a person, a second AI reviewer evaluates it for accuracy, relevance, safety, and plain readability. Suggestions that don't pass don't get sent.

It stays with you

Reminders, encouragement, and practical problem-solving when something gets in the way: a ride falls through, the weather turns, or walking in alone feels like too much.

03 The research

The study

A two-phase, mixed-methods pilot designed with older adults, not just for them.

ALCOVE is developed through focus groups and iterative co-design workshops with older adults and community partners, then tested in a single-arm pilot with approximately 50 community-dwelling adults aged 65 and over across North Florida. Findings will inform a future NIH-funded randomized controlled trial.

Phase 1Oct – Dec 2026

Listening first

Focus groups with older adults on the real barriers to social and volunteer participation. A Community Advisory Board is established, and construction of the community activity database begins.

Phase 2Dec 2026 – Mar 2027

Designing together

Four iterative co-design workshops shape the system's features, tone, and recommendations. The AI system is built and tested internally against synthetic user profiles, followed by a usability evaluation with older adults.

Phase 3Apr 2027 – Mar 2028

The pilot

Approximately 50 participants use ALCOVE over six months on their own phone or tablet. Weekly check-ins track activity, mood, social connection, and barriers, alongside assessments at the start, three months, and six months.

Phase 4Apr – Sep 2028

Sharing what we learn

Analysis of outcomes in loneliness, social engagement, and emotional well-being; publications and conference presentations; and accessible summary reports delivered back to the community partners who made the work possible.

06 Common questions

Questions people ask

Who can take part in the study?

Adults aged 65 and over living in the North Florida study area, who live alone or are at elevated risk of social isolation, and who have access to a smartphone or tablet. Basic training on using the app is provided, you don't need to be comfortable with technology already.

When does enrollment open?

Pilot enrollment is expected to begin in spring 2027, after the co-design and development phase is complete. Recruitment will run on a rolling basis through partner organizations. This page will be updated when enrollment opens.

Does it cost anything to participate?

No. Participation is free, and enrolled participants receive incentives for completing study assessments.

Is ALCOVE meant to replace human contact?

The opposite. ALCOVE's entire purpose is to get people out of the house and into rooms with other people. It is a way of finding and getting to real, in-person community activity and not a substitute for it.

How is participant information handled?

ALCOVE is a research study. Study procedures will be reviewed and approved by the Florida State University Institutional Review Board before enrollment begins. Participants stay anonymous to community organizations unless and until they choose to express interest in a specific activity, and a study coordinator manages that introduction. Participants receive full details about data collection and use during the informed consent process.

My organization runs activities for older adults. Can we be listed?

Yes. Community organizations, senior centers, nonprofits, and faith-based groups in North Florida are invited to contribute and validate activity listings. Get in touch using the contact details below.
